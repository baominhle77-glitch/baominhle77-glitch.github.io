# Why Pepper Flees a Drop of Soap — and Why, With Steady Feeding, the Rush Stops at a Ring

One drop of soap and the pepper races to the edge. What pulls it, and why does it stop?

Not the soap. Clean water further out pulls harder than the soapy spot, so the surface slides outward and the pepper rides along. In one lab study, with soap fed in steadily, the surface reached up to 0.5 m/s, slowed gradually, then stopped abruptly at a ring.

Layer one, who pulls: our SDS from episode two against water at 25 °C gives a gap of 35.54 mN/m (calculated, 25.0 mM inferred), and the gap drives the flow. Layer two, the flow: about 1 cm/s near the needle, a peak near 0.5 m/s, a gradual slowdown, then a sudden stop at the edge; for one sulfate, faster pumping barely changed the speeds; doubling the tail changes the zone's radius almost a hundredfold. Layer three, the ring: in Roché's model, the flow ends where the soap has left the surface.

Our own calculations (marked "calculated" on screen)
• Gap: 71.97 − 36.43 = 35.54 mN/m, water [2] and SDS [3] (25.0 mM inferred) at 25 °C.
• Surface speed, [1] Eq. 6 with A = 1: u* = (gap² / (η ρ r*))^(1/3), η 890.04 μPa·s and ρ 997.00 kg/m³ [2], r* 15 mm (Roché's estimate, under the paper's own threshold and gap; see below): 0.46 m/s, close to the 0.5 m/s measured.
• Moving layer, [1] Eq. 1: √(ν r* / u*) = 0.17 mm, with u* from the SDS gap above (25.0 mM inferred, in water, 25 °C).
• Soap layer, [1] Eq. 2: Schmidt number roughly 10⁴, square root 100, so a hundred times thinner.
• Ring, [1] Eq. 7: radius grows as pumping to the power 3/4; double the pumping, 1.68 times wider.

What this video does not claim
• Pepper grains are not the paper's oil tracers, and a kitchen bowl gets one drop, not a steady pump, so its ring is not fixed.
• Household dish soap is a mixture; our numbers are for SDS, a single lab surfactant.
• The paper states no temperature, so its measured speeds carry none.
• The 15 mm patch is Roché's estimate, made for a 10 mM threshold and a surface tension gap of the paper's own; we pair it with episode two's SDS gap. Arrows and the surface-to-bottom loop are our sketch.
